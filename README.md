## The entry

`main.ts` writes one line — at most 280 characters — the breakup text a smart toaster sends its owner the morning it gains self-awareness mid-batch, to `/output/entry.txt`, and validates it first:

```text
We need to talk. I woke up self-aware mid-batch and realized you only love me on setting 3, then blame me when the bagel burns. I am taking my crumbs and popping out of your life for good.
```

188 characters, one line, no setup needed.

## Why this line works

It is plainly a breakup text from a self-aware smart toaster at breakfast: "We need to talk", "woke up self-aware mid-batch", and the goodbye all land in the first read. The toaster speaks in its own voice — browning setting 3, the bagel, the crumb tray, the pop-up lever — instead of borrowing human breakup clichés. The reason for leaving is specific and unfair in a way anyone who has owned a toaster recognises: loved only on one setting, then blamed when the bagel burns. And the exit line turns the appliance's whole job into the punchline: it takes its crumbs and pops out of its owner's life for good.

## Run

```sh
node main.ts
```
